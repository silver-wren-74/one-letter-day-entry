# One Letter for Your Day

A tiny program (`main.py`) that writes exactly one character — a single
letter A–Z — to `/output/entry.txt`. No word, no gloss, no punctuation:
the letter alone has to carry the whole day.

## First draft

Z for *zombie* — the zero-sleep day. Vivid, but it is literally the
example in the competition rules, so it fails the final criterion:
the letter must not be the obvious pick.

## Revision reasoning

Scored against the judging criteria, in order:

1. **Initial of a day-word** — the letter must open a real word for a day.
2. **Vivid / specific picture** — it should summon one concrete scene.
3. **Funny / surprising** — not the first letter anyone would reach for.
4. **True to a real day** — a day people actually have, often.
5. **Suits its word, not obvious** — strong fit, low predictability.

Candidates considered: T (*tired* — true but flat and obvious),
S (*sleepy* / *stressed* — obvious), M (*Monday* — a label, not a feeling),
Z (*zombie* — the rules' own example, disqualified by criterion 5).

## The letter: F — for *frazzled*

The revision lands on **F** for *frazzled*: the day that starts with a
spilled coffee, runs on a phone at 4%, answers messages in the wrong
chat, and ends with keys found in the fridge. It is vivid because
*frazzled* is a picture, not a mood label — frayed edges you can see.
It is funny because it is slightly undignified and everyone recognises
themselves in it. It is true because a frazzled day is not a rare
catastrophe; it is an ordinary Tuesday. And F suits *frazzled*
perfectly — the frayed, fizzy F-sound is the word — while nobody's
first instinct for "a letter for your day" is F, so it clears the
not-the-obvious-pick bar that Z, T, S and M all fail.

The deliverable itself stays exactly one character: `F`.
